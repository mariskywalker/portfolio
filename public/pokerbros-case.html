01, Context

A GLOBAL PLATFORM.
ONE VISUAL VOICE.

PokerBros is a social poker platform with millions of players across dozens of markets. The product exists not in a single context, but across an entire ecosystem of touchpoints: native mobile app, web platform, content operation, podcast programming, live events, and branded physical environments.

The visual identity needed to carry meaning and consistency without relying on language, hold across radically different formats and scales simultaneously, and feel native to each medium while remaining unmistakably the same brand.

03, Design Approach

THE STRATEGY
BEHIND THE SYSTEM.

Every decision was guided by a small set of strategic principles applied consistently across every format, scale, and context the brand occupied.

Recognition before aesthetics

In a fragmented media environment, the first job of a visual identity is to be recognisable, not just attractive. Every decision was made with one question: will this read as PokerBros, immediately, at any scale, in any context?

Clarity under density

Poker, as a game and as a culture, is dense with information. The design system needed to impose clarity, to make hierarchy legible, navigation fast, and friction low, without sacrificing visual personality.

Bold, not aggressive

The category defaults to visual aggression. PokerBros was built to be bold and confident without being confrontational, warmth and energy over dominance. That distinction shaped every colour, typographic, and illustration choice.

Consistency as structure

Consistency wasn't enforced by rules that said everything must look the same. It was achieved by building a system with enough internal logic that coherent decisions emerged naturally, even in contexts the original guidelines didn't anticipate.

Flexibility within structure

A rigid identity breaks under pressure. Clear rules where consistency is non-negotiable (colour, typography, logo usage), genuine expressive range where variety is appropriate (illustration, content formats, motion).

Brand as product

Brand and product were never treated as separate domains. The app UI was not a separate visual system from the brand identity, it was an extension of it. Every product interaction reinforced brand recognition.

04, Execution

WHAT WAS
BUILT.

The work covered every context the brand occupied each with its own formal logic, all part of the same system.

Brand System
Identity & Logobook

The full visual identity: primary mark and all approved variants (stacked, horizontal, icon-only, line-only), a five-colour palette with clearly defined usage rules, a typographic system anchored in Impact for display and Helvetica Neue for body, a 3D illustration direction with custom characters, and a comprehensive brand guidelines document codifying every decision, not just what, but why.

Product
App & Web UI System

A UI system built to support a complex, information-dense poker experience while remaining visually coherent with the brand. Component logic and brand personality were never in tension, each reinforced the other. The web extended this into a broader editorial context with different layouts and content types while maintaining full visual continuity.

Content
Podcast, Editorial & Social

Each content format received its own formal treatment, thumbnail systems, cover art, episode artwork, social templates, designed for its medium and optimised for its distribution context. The social visual system enabled high-frequency content production without degrading brand quality, with format-specific rules for feed, story, carousel, and reels.

Physical
Merch, Events & Venue

Physical applications tested the identity's structural integrity, how it performs without digital rendering, at scale, on materials, and in three dimensions. Merchandise, poker chips, table felt, venue signage, and event materials each had specific rules derived from the same underlying system.

05, Impact

A SYSTEM THAT
HOLDS AT SCALE.

"The most meaningful measure is not a metric
it is a structural quality."

PokerBros now has a visual language that can be applied by different people, in different contexts, at different scales, and still feel like the same brand. That is what a design system is supposed to do, and it is harder to achieve than it looks.

The brand is more legible, more consistent, and better equipped to scale than it was before. The systems built here will outlast any single campaign or content cycle.

Global recognition

A visual fingerprint that cannot be confused with a competitor, readable in any format, at any scale, in any market.

Cross-platform cohesion

Full visual consistency across app, web, content, media, and physical environments, without fragmenting.

Scalable infrastructure

A documented system that enables others to produce on-brand work without degrading quality, even at high frequency.

Brand as product

The product UI reinforces brand recognition in every interaction, brand and product are the same visual world.
